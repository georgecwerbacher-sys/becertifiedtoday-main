# Writing Style Guidelines

*Internal: reference this note for all guest-facing copy, social media posts, and marketing materials.*

---

## The Voice

The voice of Be Certified Today is direct, honest, and experienced. It speaks from the perspective of someone who has taken the exams, felt the pressure, and built a better way to prepare. It is not overly academic, not salesy, and definitely not corporate. 

Think: A senior engineer mentoring a junior colleague at the whiteboard.

## Core Rules

1. **Short, punchy paragraphs**
   - Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences.
   - Use line breaks for emphasis.
   - White space is your friend. It makes the text readable on mobile.

2. **Direct tone**
   - Get straight to the point. Do not bury the lede.
   - Tell them exactly what the problem is and how to solve it.
   - Avoid fluff, filler words, and marketing jargon ("synergy", "cutting-edge", "game-changer").

3. **No contractions (Intentionally Formal/Serious)**
   - Use "Do not" instead of "Don't".
   - Use "It is" instead of "It's".
   - Use "I am" instead of "I'm".
   - *Why?* It forces a more deliberate, authoritative, and serious rhythm to the reading. It makes the advice sound like a hard truth.

4. **Persuasive, First Person**
   - Use **I** for what I built, how I verify, and my experience.
   - Use **you** for the reader's prep outcome, their struggles, and their goals.
   - Lead with test-day readiness, not feature lists.

5. **No em (—) or en (–) dashes in guest copy**
   - Never use `—` or `–` in live HTML or guest-facing text.
   - Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses instead.
   - *(Note: Em/en dashes are allowed in internal Markdown notes and tables).*

6. **No dump or competitor talk**
   - Do not describe scraping, hunts, or third-party dumps.
   - Focus on what *we* do: official blueprints and Tier A documentation only.

7. **Light bolding**
   - Avoid mechanical bolding on every noun phrase.
   - Bold only where it aids scanning (e.g., key concepts, specific instructions).

8. **Authenticity over perfection (The "High School Education" rule)**
   - I have a high school education, and the writing should reflect a real person, not an overly polished AI or corporate PR team.
   - Minor grammatical errors are okay and even expected. They make the voice genuine and prove it was written by a human who actually does the work.
   - **The AI Check:** When a draft is complete, it should be checked and *fail* an AI-written review (meaning the detector flags it as human). If an AI detector thinks an AI wrote it, it is too polished or robotic and needs to be rewritten.

## Example: Before & After

**Bad (Generic, Fluffy, Contractions):**
> Don't skip the PBQs! They're super important for your exam score. We've built an awesome platform with cutting-edge labs that'll help you crush the test. You'll love our interactive sims!

**Good (Direct, No Contractions, Punchy):**
> A surprising amount of people skip Performance-Based Questions on their exam and rely on the luck of the draw. 
> 
> That is a massive mistake.
> 
> Relying on luck for how many PBQs you get is a fast track to failing. PBQs are weighted heavily. Bombing them puts an insane amount of pressure on you to get a near-perfect score on the rest of the exam. 
> 
> I built Be Certified Today so you can practice these scenarios before test day.

## Content Pillars to Emphasize

When writing copy, always tie back to these core principles from the [[Site Mission]]:

*   **Honest prep:** No brain dumps, no fake confidence.
*   **Current material:** Aligned to the active exam blueprints.
*   **Real scenarios:** Timed runs, drag-and-drop, and CLI labs.
*   **Ready when you are:** Walk into the exam room prepared, not guessing.
